# Task 3 — Object detection from a dataset you built yourself
Build a model that identifies an object over a camera — real-time video or a single image, your choice.

**The object doesn't matter.** Pens, mugs, faces, footpath cracks, your keys — whatever is in front of you. Pick something convenient.

**The model doesn't much matter either.** Fine-tune something existing, or design your own. If you want to build the architecture yourself we'd genuinely like to see that.

**What matters is the data, and there is one hard requirement: the dataset must be one you acquired yourself.** Not a downloaded, pre-labelled, pre-split benchmark. You collect it, you label it, you split it. That is the part of machine learning that every tutorial skips and every real project lives or dies on, and it's the part we want to see you do.

**Show us:**

- **Your training set** — how many samples, how you collected them, how you labelled them and with what tool
- **Your test set** — how you split it, and why you split it that way
- **Accuracy** — and whether accuracy is the right measure for what you built. If it isn't, tell us what is and use that instead.
- **Where it fails** — what does it get wrong, and do you know why?
- **A live demo** at the debrief

**Optional, only if you have time:** wire the three together — detect something, work out where it is, put it on a map. Don't sacrifice the first three tasks for this.
